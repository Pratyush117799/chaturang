The Great Comparison

Chaturanga vs Chess vs Xiangqi

Three ancient strategy games. One shared ancestor. How do they compare — and which should you play?

♟

Chaturanga

India · ~6th Century CE

The original. Dice-based, 4-player capable, probabilistic strategy on an 8×8 board. The ancestor of all three games below.

♔

Chess

Europe · ~15th Century CE

The world's most played strategy game. Deterministic, deeply studied, with centuries of theory. Evolved from Shatranj via medieval Europe.

將

Xiangqi

China · ~10th–12th Century CE

Chinese Chess. Played by hundreds of millions. Unique mechanics — the River, the Palace, the Cannon — distinguish it sharply from its siblings.

Deep Dive

The Dice — Chaturanga's Defining Feature

The single biggest difference between Chaturanga and every other member of the chess family is the Pasha — the dice. In Chess and Xiangqi, players have perfect information and complete freedom of choice: on any given turn you can move any legal piece. In Chaturanga, the dice decides which piece type you may move.

This transforms strategy entirely. Where chess rewards deep calculation of forced sequences (if I move here, you must respond there, then I play…), Chaturanga rewards positional flexibility. You must build a board position that gives you good options regardless of what the dice produces — because you can never guarantee you'll get to move the piece you most want to move.

Skilled Chaturanga players develop an eye for redundancy and ensemble strength: arrangements where several different piece types are simultaneously threatening or well-placed. This probabilistic strategic thinking is genuinely different from anything found in modern chess or Xiangqi.

Which Should You Play?

Play Chaturanga if you're curious about chess's origins, enjoy probabilistic strategy games, or want a genuinely unique experience unlike any other game. Its dice mechanic makes it more forgiving for beginners (a bad position can be rescued by good dice) while rewarding deeper positional thinking at high levels.

Play Chess if you want the deepest competitive ecosystem, the most developed opening theory, and the richest library of resources, puzzles, and grandmaster games.

Play Xiangqi if you want a fresh strategic challenge with chess-like rules but distinctly different tactics — the Cannon and the River create entirely different patterns of play that reward creativity.

Ideally? Play all three. Understanding Chaturanga makes you appreciate where Chess and Xiangqi came from. Playing Xiangqi gives you new chess intuitions. And returning to Chaturanga with chess knowledge makes you a stronger Chaturanga player too.
