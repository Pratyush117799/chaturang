Prototype 1Dhanurdhari Gaja — धनुर्धारी गज
Archer War Elephant  ·  16-block meander ring  ·  Trunk RAISED in battle cry
The trunk raised upward is the battle-cry pose — the most dramatic and iconic posture in Indian war elephant imagery. An armoured caparison covers the elephant's body with decorative panel detailing. A turbaned mahout sits on the neck with an ankush (goad). An archer stands in the howdah, bow fully drawn with arrow nocked, string pulled to cheek. At 32 px the raised trunk arc + howdah box are immediately readable.

Prototype 2Chatri Gaja — छत्री गज
Royal Canopy Elephant  ·  8-petal royal lotus ring  ·  Trunk DOWN, ornate howdah + chatri dome
The royal ceremonial war elephant, directly referencing the carved ivory chess pieces in the reference images. Trunk gently curled downward (dignity rather than aggression). A full ornate howdah seat with two riders visible behind its carved rail. A chatri parasol dome rises above the howdah on a pole — the most architecturally complex silhouette of the three. Elaborate multi-tier caparison with chain fringe along the bottom edge.

Prototype 3Veera Gaja — वीर गज
Battle Elephant  ·  24-spoke Ashoka ring  ·  Trunk FORWARD, forehead armour, war flag
The most aggressive posture — trunk thrust forward as a weapon, the elephant's own body becoming the battering ram. A forehead armour plate (gaja kavach) is bolted to the elephant's crown. A single warrior mahout on the neck holds a spear. A compact battle howdah has a dhvaja war flag flying from its post. This is the most martial silhouette: elephant body + forward trunk + raised flag = war machine. The 24-spoke Ashoka ring is the sovereign battle emblem.
